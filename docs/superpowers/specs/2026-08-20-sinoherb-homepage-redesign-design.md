# SinoHerb Homepage Redesign Design

## Goal

Build a fresh, premium Chinese herbal wellness homepage inspired by the reference site’s business category and content rhythm, but with a distinct visual system and page architecture.

The new page should feel like a modern, credible brand site: calm, editorial, product-led, and easier to scan than the reference.

## Visual Direction

- Palette: off-white base, deep plum type, muted sage accents, pale blush surfaces, small warm copper highlights.
- Type: elegant serif for headlines, clean sans-serif for supporting copy and UI chrome.
- Mood: premium, clinical, calm, trustworthy.
- Layout: spacious, modular, asymmetric where helpful, with a stronger product/story hierarchy than the reference.
- Motion: subtle fades, gentle hover lift, small scroll reveals only.

## Page Structure

1. Top announcement bar with a short trust message.
2. Header with logo, core nav, and one primary action.
3. Hero section with a strong headline, short supporting copy, CTA pair, and product imagery.
4. Trust strip with press / proof / credibility markers.
5. Product highlight section with 3 featured formula cards.
6. Ingredient story section with large imagery and concise benefit points.
7. How it works section with 3 steps and supporting explanation.
8. Results section with metric cards and testimonial excerpts.
9. Founder/story section with a more editorial full-width treatment.
10. Email signup and footer links.

## Component Set

- Announcement bar
- Header/nav
- Primary and secondary buttons
- Hero content block
- Product cards
- Ingredient cards
- Step cards
- Metric cards
- Testimonial cards
- Story section
- Signup form
- Footer link grid

## Content Rules

- Use SinoHerb as the brand.
- Keep copy new and brand-specific, not copied from the reference.
- Preserve the general category of content: Chinese herbal wellness, formulas, ingredients, process, results, founder story.
- Avoid the reference site’s exact layout patterns, especially the right-side cart drawer and nearly identical headline composition.

## Behavior

- Buttons and cards should have subtle hover states.
- On smaller screens, sections should stack cleanly and keep the first viewport strong.
- No new interaction should block reading or product discovery.

## Success Criteria

- The page feels clearly inspired by the reference but is visually its own design.
- The first viewport has strong brand signal and clear CTA hierarchy.
- Content remains readable and balanced at desktop and mobile widths.
- The final implementation feels production-ready, not like a rough template.
